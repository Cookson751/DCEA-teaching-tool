Why these defaults differ from older DCEA calculators

Current defaults: England QALE for 2017–18, England population for 2023, and precisely equal 20% IMD quintiles.

1. Baseline QALE. The default 2017–18 England estimates use EQ-5D-5L quality adjustment and are reported in Table 2 of Love-Koh et al. (2023). They imply a most-to-least-deprived QALE gap of about 11.1 QALYs. The older DCEA web calculator used 2011 estimates based on EQ-5D-3L from Love-Koh et al. (2015), stored in qale_pop_imd.csv, giving a gap of about 11.9 QALYs. The DCEA handbook used slightly different 2011 estimates because of a different QALE modelling approach. These differences have little bearing on the slope-index measure of absolute inequality when the gap is similar, but they do affect the Atkinson index and therefore equity-weighted NHB and inequality-adjusted ICER results.

2. Population. The default is the ONS 2023 England mid-year population estimate. This is about 4.5 million higher than the 2011 total used in the old DCEA web calculator and about 2.4 million higher than the 2016 estimate used in the DCEA handbook and supplementary material to Love-Koh et al. (2020).

3. Quintile shares. This tool uses exactly 20% of the population in each quintile, like the old web calculator. The DCEA handbook used slightly unequal shares based on ONS data around 2016, and the old web-tool repository also contains qale_pop_imd old.csv with unequal shares. Small departures from 20% arise because populations grow unevenly within the fixed LSOA boundaries used to define IMD groups.

Sources: Love-Koh et al. (2023), ONS population estimates, and the earlier DCEA calculator code/data at github.com/bitowaqr/dcea. ONS IMD population series: population by IMD, 2001–2019.

Methods and definitions

What the tool is calculating

Health inequality impact (HII)

The tool regresses group net health benefit on the fixed quintile fractional ranks 0.1–0.9. HII is −0.8 times the slope coefficient. Positive HII therefore means a reduction in the predicted QALY gap between the most and least deprived groups.

Inequality-adjusted ICER and HIM

Atkinson marginal equity weights are normalised to mean 1 and applied directly to gross health benefits and financial opportunity costs. The health inequality modifier (HIM) is the conventional ICER divided by the inequality-adjusted ICER.

Critical inequality aversion

Critical ε* is found numerically from the value of ε at which directly equity-weighted NHB equals zero. This avoids confusing an inequality-aversion “threshold” with the cost-effectiveness threshold k.

Indirect EDE method

The advanced results also calculate Atkinson equally distributed equivalent (EDE) health before the intervention, after gross health benefit, and after net health benefit. Full precision is retained internally; rounding is applied only to displayed values.

Equity–efficiency plane terminology

When “equity-weighted QALYs” is selected for the x-axis, the plotted quantity is the equity benefit: directly equity-weighted NHB minus unweighted NHB. This matches the logic of the earlier workbook impact plane while making the horizontal axis a distributional increment rather than another overall decision metric.

About this tool

Acknowledgements, disclaimer and references

About

This standalone HTML teaching tool is a development of the University of York health inequality impact calculators for England. It supports simple aggregate distributional cost-effectiveness analysis (DCEA) of the impact of an intervention on health inequality and potential trade-offs with conventional cost effectiveness, using user-supplied intervention inputs.

The earlier spreadsheet version was designed so users could see and modify the underlying calculations. This HTML version keeps the calculation engine transparent while providing a cleaner teaching interface, updated terminology and interactive sensitivity analysis.
